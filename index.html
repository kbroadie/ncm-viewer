Next-Concept Modeling writes text the way you'd sketch a drawing: a rough outline, then a few passes of refinement, with every position in a pass filled at once. These figures show how that works, using the published samples and the model configs from the NCM repo.

The NCM lane plays the actual WMT14 sample from Fig. 1. The other two lanes are simulations of the same final sentence. Real autoregressive models take one pass per token, which is more than one per word, so that lane understates them. The diffusion lane uses 128 steps, the step count of the diffusion baselines in the paper's LM1B and XSum comparisons. Pass counts aren't wall-clock time: passes differ in cost, and the paper measures time separately.

From the paper: passes per sample

Speedups are the paper's wall-clock comparisons against the strongest diffusion model at matched scale. NCM's quality was better on LM1B, competitive on OpenWebText, and matched on XSum ROUGE-L.

Samples transcribed from HazyResearch/ncm (MIT license). Level schedules come from the repo's config/eval files. Highlights are computed here by a word diff against the previous level.
